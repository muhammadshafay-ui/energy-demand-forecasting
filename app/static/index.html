📖 About This Project

Electricity grids can't store large amounts of power — supply has to match demand in real time. Utility operators need to know, in advance, how much electricity a region will consume so they can schedule power generation, avoid overloading the grid, and prevent unnecessary (expensive) emergency power purchases. This tool forecasts hourly electricity demand for the PJM East grid — a real regional transmission network covering part of the Eastern United States — using 16 years of historical consumption data and a machine learning model trained to recognize daily, weekly, and seasonal demand patterns.

🏭

Grid Stability

Accurate demand forecasts help operators avoid blackouts and brownouts by ensuring enough power is generated at the right time — not too little, not too much.

💰

Cost Efficiency

Buying emergency power during unexpected demand spikes is expensive. Forecasting ahead lets utilities plan cheaper, scheduled generation instead.

🌱

Sustainability

Better demand planning reduces wasted energy production and supports smoother integration of renewable sources, which depend on accurate load matching.

📊

Data-Driven Planning

Replaces guesswork with a model trained on real historical patterns — daily peaks, weekday/weekend shifts, and summer/winter seasonality.

🧠 About the Model

Algorithm

XGBoost Regressor — trained on 16 years of hourly consumption data (2002-2018)

Features Used

Time-based (hour, day, month, season) + lag features (1h, 24h, 168h ago) + rolling averages

Forecasting Method

Recursive multi-step forecasting — each predicted hour feeds into the next hour's prediction

Validation

Time-aware train/test split (no shuffling) — tested only on data chronologically after training data
